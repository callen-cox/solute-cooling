The idea

Small data centers often have no room for a radiator: an office building's server closet, a cramped site in a developing region, or a relay in orbit. This controller treats the coolant as a finite thermal sink and manages it autonomously, with a safety supervisor that can always overrule it.

Three layers, every control step

1 · Safety supervisor (highest priority) overrides everything: NORMAL → WARNING → THROTTLE → SHUTDOWN.
2 · Optimisation mode Efficiency, Performance or Stealth decides how to spend the cooling.
3 · Cooling sink Salt dissolution, PCM modules or orbital radiators do the physical work.

Off: clean shutdown, latched until the coolant is back under 46 °C. Coolant: warn 52, cut 56, off 60 °C (Tsafe), loop hardware limit 85 °C. Chips: warn 80, cut 85, off 90 °C, damage 100 °C. The config refuses any setting with under 10 °C of margin. In orbit a freeze guard also protects the bottom of the range.

Every step

  1. Sense: three sensors are median-voted, so a broken one is outvoted. Each is also compared with the others over about 30 seconds, so one that slowly drifts is dropped before it can fool the vote. With no usable data it assumes the worst and shuts down.
  2. Predict: the trend is projected 60 s ahead (10 min in orbit). For the salt, a learned model of the room predicts 5 minutes ahead (below).
  3. Supervise: the ladders above, for the coolant and the hottest chip. Running low on cooling escalates early. Running out triggers a graceful power-down.
  4. Act: the mode drives the sink (below).

The brain: what makes it smart

  • It learns the room. It starts from the design sheet (25 kg of water, a 10 W/K leak to the room) and learns the real heat capacity, leak and heat absorbed per gram from its own sensor data, using recursive least squares (a Kalman filter). The Learned room model tile shows each value with its uncertainty (the simulator's true values are 20 kg, 15 W/K and 277 J/g). Water and J/g lock in within minutes. The leak stays uncertain until the temperature swings (try a scheduled job), because a steady room can't reveal it.
  • It plans ahead (model-predictive control). Every 5 s it simulates the next 5 minutes for dozens of dosing rates and picks the one that keeps the coolant in its band for the least salt. When a job is scheduled, it decides to pre-cool on its own.
  • It catches bad sensors, both sudden failures (outvoted) and slow drift (dropped before it matters).
  • It learns the pump. If the pump delivers less than asked, it notices and compensates.
  • It watches the chips, not just the water. A clogged cold plate can cook a chip while the coolant looks fine, so it projects the chips' trend, sheds load before they reach their limit, and won't go back to full power while that would overheat them.

The three cooling sinks

SinkPhysicsWhat the controller does
Salt
terrestrial		Dissolving NH4Cl absorbs 277 J/g (ΔHsoln = +14.8 kJ/mol), like an instant cold pack. It is consumed: about 13 kg per kWh. Doses grams per second by model-predictive control, learns the pump and the room, and tracks saturation against the coldest planned temperature, because NH4Cl is less soluble when cold. At 85% it swaps to a reserve tank. With no reserve it stops dosing before crystals clog the cold plates.
PCM
terrestrial		Sodium thiosulfate pentahydrate melts at 48.3 °C and absorbs about 200 kJ/kg doing it, then re-solidifies to be reused. Runs one module at a time and swaps when it is spent. When the modules are half spent it schedules a low-power state so idle ones re-solidify.
Radiators
orbital		No air in space, so heat only leaves as radiation: Q = εσAT⁴. Sunlight adds αSA·cos θ. Keeps the radiators edge-on to the sun. If they absorb more than they emit (stuck facing the sun), it retracts them behind the MLI shield. A freeze guard (heater, sun-facing, retraction) keeps the water above 5 °C.

Where it runs

  • On a laptop: start.bat runs the Python control loop as a FastAPI server, and this page streams from it.
  • Online: with no server, this page loads Python into your browser (Pyodide, WebAssembly) and runs the exact same coolant/core.py.
  • On real hardware: swap the simulator for a HAL class that reads real sensors and drives a real pump, radiators or server power caps.
